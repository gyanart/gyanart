## Research question

When a business owner says their business is “not showing on Google Maps,” what different situations could they mean, how can they distinguish them, and what safe next steps does Google recommend?

## Key finding: distinguish absence from low visibility

Do not assume every case is an SEO ranking problem. The guide should first distinguish:
1. The owner cannot find or manage the profile while signed into the correct Google account.
2. The profile is not publicly visible because it is unverified, disabled, suspended, or otherwise ineligible.
3. The profile exists, but does not appear for a particular service/category query or in a particular area.
4. The profile was recently created or edited and Google has not yet reflected the change in results.

These are distinct situations and require different checks.

## Source-backed findings

### 1. Verify the profile and search correctly
Google says only verified businesses can show their business information on Search and Maps. It recommends searching for the business name plus city, or “my business,” while signed into the Google Account associated with the profile.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Find your business on Google”  
https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585/find-your-business-on-google?hl=en

### 2. A newly created profile or recent edit may need time
Google says rankings for new businesses can take up to a month to appear in search results. Changes to business information can take up to three days to appear. Avoid promising an immediate fix or implying that a delay alone proves a penalty.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Find your business on Google”  
https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585/find-your-business-on-google?hl=en

### 3. Check for suspension, disabling or policy issues
Google says a profile that violates its guidelines may be disabled or suspended and will not show to other users until the issue is resolved. Owners should check the account for notifications and follow Google's resolution/appeal process when applicable. Do not recommend creating a duplicate profile to get around a suspension.

Sources:
- https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585/find-your-business-on-google?hl=en
- https://support.google.com/business/answer/13762416?hl=en-11
- https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en

### 4. Eligibility matters
Google says a business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours to qualify, with specific exceptions. Online-only businesses and lead-generation companies are among examples of ineligible profiles. A service-area business may qualify if it visits or delivers to customers; eligibility is not the same as having a storefront.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Business eligibility and ownership guidelines”  
https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en

### 5. Local ranking depends on relevance, distance and prominence
Google identifies relevance, distance and prominence as the main factors in local results. It recommends complete, accurate business information. Google also says there is no way to request or pay for better organic local ranking. Explain these factors in plain language; do not present them as a complete or guaranteed ranking formula.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Tips to improve your local ranking on Google”  
https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en

### 6. Add or claim the profile when appropriate
If no profile exists, Google provides a process to add one. If an unverified profile already exists, the owner may be able to claim it and complete verification. If another person owns the verified profile, use Google's ownership process rather than creating a duplicate.

Source: Google Business Profile Help, “Add or claim your Business Profile”  
https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en

## Proposed diagnostic flow for the eventual article

1. Search the exact business name plus city in Google Maps and Google Search while signed into the account that manages the profile.
2. Check whether the profile exists and whether the owner can access it.
3. Check verification status and any suspension/disabled-profile notices.
4. Confirm the business meets eligibility and representation guidelines.
5. If the profile is live but not visible for a service search, explain relevance, distance and prominence; test a few relevant searches from different locations without promising a fixed ranking.
6. Check whether the profile or key details were created/edited recently and allow for Google's stated processing windows.
7. Only then work through profile completeness, correct categories/services, accurate hours/contact details, reviews and relevant website information.
8. Escalate suspension, ownership and verification problems through Google's official processes.
