# Why Is My Business Not Showing on Google Maps? 7 Checks to Try

You search for your business on Google Maps, but it does not appear. Or perhaps your business shows up when you search its name, but not when someone searches for the service you provide.

These can be different problems. Before changing your profile or paying someone to “fix your rankings,” work out which situation you are dealing with.

This guide walks through seven checks you can do yourself. It uses Google's published guidance and does not promise a particular ranking or outcome.

## First, identify what “not showing” means

Start by choosing the symptom that best describes your situation:

- **You cannot find the business even by its exact name.** Check whether a profile exists, whether you are signed into the right Google account, and whether verification or a policy issue needs attention.
- **You can find it by name, but not for a service search.** For example, a bakery appears when you search its name but not when you search “bakery near me.” The profile may be visible, while its ranking for that broader query is different.
- **It appears in some searches or areas but not others.** Local results can vary with the searcher's location and the relevance of the query.

Do not assume all three situations have the same cause. Work through the checks below in order.

## Check 1: Search for your exact business name and city

Open Google Maps and search for your business's actual name along with its city or locality. Also search for the name in Google Search.

If you manage the profile, sign in to the Google Account associated with it and search for your business or “my business.” This helps you access the management view and see whether Google is asking you to take action.

If useful, check the public result in a private/incognito browser window as well. This is a comparison, not a perfect ranking test: results can still depend on location and other context.

**What to do next**
- If you find the profile by name, note that it is discoverable for that search. Continue to Check 6 if it is missing only for service/category searches.
- If you cannot find it at all, continue to Check 2. Do not create another profile yet.

Official guidance: [Find your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en)

## Check 2: Confirm whether a profile already exists and who controls it

A business may already have a listing even if the current owner did not create it. Search carefully before adding a new one.

- **No profile appears:** First check that your business is eligible for a Google Business Profile. If it is, follow Google's process to add one.
- **An existing profile is unverified:** Follow the official process to claim it and complete verification.
- **Another person owns the verified profile:** Request access or follow Google's ownership process.
- **You see a duplicate or matching listing:** Resolve the existing profile rather than creating another one. Google says duplicate profiles may not appear in Search or Maps.

Creating extra profiles to try to improve visibility can make the situation more confusing and is not a reliable fix.

Official guidance:
- [Add or claim your Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/2911778?hl=en)
- [Resolve duplicate profiles and ownership issues](https://support.google.com/business/answer/12756178?hl=en)

## Check 3: Check whether the profile is verified

Google says only verified businesses can show their business information on Search and Maps. If verification is incomplete, the profile may not be visible to customers.

Open the profile using the Google Account that manages it and check for a verification prompt or status message. Complete the method Google offers for your profile. Available verification methods can vary, so follow the instructions shown in your account rather than relying on a method someone else used.

After completing verification, check the profile status and allow Google's process to finish. Avoid repeatedly changing details or creating replacement profiles while you are waiting.

Official guidance: [Verify your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7107242?hl=en)

## Check 4: Confirm eligibility and look for policy or suspension notices

Not every business qualifies for a Google Business Profile. In general, the business needs to have in-person contact with customers during its stated hours, subject to Google's specific rules. A business does not necessarily need a customer-facing storefront: some service-area businesses qualify when they visit or deliver to customers. Online-only businesses are generally not eligible.

Make sure the profile represents the real business accurately. Check the business name, address or service-area setup, category and other details against Google's guidelines.

Then look for notices that say the profile is disabled or suspended. If Google has restricted the profile, read the stated reason and follow the official resolution or appeal process. Prepare relevant supporting documents if requested. Do not create a replacement profile to get around a suspension, and do not submit repeated appeals for the same issue while one is pending.

**Important:** a suspension or eligibility issue needs a different response from ordinary ranking competition. A third-party consultant cannot guarantee that Google will reinstate a profile.

Official guidance:
- [Business eligibility and ownership guidelines](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13763036?hl=en)
- [Fix a suspended or disabled Business Profile](https://support.google.com/business/answer/4569145?hl=en)
- [Appeal Business Profile content and profile restrictions](https://support.google.com/business/answer/13597551?hl=en)

## Check 5: Consider whether the profile or its information was recently added or changed

If you recently created a profile or edited key information, the change may not appear immediately. Google says rankings for new businesses can take up to a month to appear in search results, and edits to business information can take up to three days to appear.

These are possible processing windows, not a promise that a profile will rank within a fixed time. A profile that remains missing after that period may have another issue, such as incomplete verification, a policy restriction, a duplicate or low visibility for a particular query.

Check the status shown in your account and avoid making repeated, speculative edits just because the result has not changed straight away.

Official guidance: [Find your business on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/145585?hl=en)

## Check 6: Separate profile visibility from local ranking

If your business appears when someone searches its exact name but not when they search a service or category, the profile may not be missing. It may simply not rank prominently for that particular search.

Google says local results are mainly influenced by three factors:

- **Relevance:** how well the profile matches what the person searched for. Keep the business category, services and other information accurate and specific.
- **Distance:** how far the business is from the person searching. A business may appear for someone nearby but not for someone much farther away.
- **Prominence:** how well-known the business is. Google describes signals such as information about the business across the web and reviews as relevant to prominence.

Try a small set of realistic searches that customers might use, and record the search phrase and approximate location. Avoid treating one search on one phone as proof that your business is invisible everywhere.

Google states that there is no way to request or pay for a better organic local ranking. Be cautious of anyone promising a guaranteed position on Google Maps.

Official guidance: [Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)

## Check 7: Improve the accuracy and usefulness of your profile

If your profile is eligible, verified and not restricted, review its information. Focus on making it correct and helpful for customers—not on trying to manipulate the ranking system.

Use this checklist:

- **Business name:** use the real-world business name. Do not add extra keywords or locations just to influence rankings.
- **Primary category and services:** select the category that best represents the business and list relevant services accurately.
- **Address or service area:** make sure the setup follows Google's rules for the way you serve customers. Do not display an address where customers are not served if Google's rules require it to be hidden.
- **Contact information and hours:** check the phone number, website, opening hours and special hours for accuracy.
- **Photos and details:** add authentic, current photos and complete relevant attributes where available.
- **Reviews:** ask real customers for honest feedback in accordance with Google's policies, and respond professionally. Never buy reviews or offer incentives in exchange for positive reviews.
- **Website consistency:** if you have a website, check that the business name, contact details, services and location information agree with your profile. A website can support customer trust and discovery, but do not assume that a website alone will guarantee Maps visibility.

Make changes based on a real error or missing information. Then monitor the profile rather than changing many fields repeatedly without a clear reason.

Official guidance: [Tips to improve your local ranking on Google](https://support.google.com/business/answer/7091?hl=en)

## When should you ask for help?

Use Google's official help and appeal routes for verification, ownership disputes, eligibility questions and suspended or disabled profiles.

If the profile is live but customers struggle to find it for relevant searches, a broader local visibility review may help you identify issues with profile accuracy, website information and the customer journey. No review can guarantee a ranking or a reinstatement decision.

## Quick recap

If your business is not showing on Google Maps:

1. Search for the exact name and city.
2. Check whether a profile already exists and who controls it.
3. Confirm verification.
4. Check eligibility, policy notices and suspension status.
5. Allow for recent setup or edits.
6. Work out whether the issue is missing profile information or local ranking.
7. Correct inaccurate details and use Google's official support routes when needed.

The first goal is to diagnose the problem correctly. That is more useful than repeatedly editing the profile or paying for a promised ranking.
