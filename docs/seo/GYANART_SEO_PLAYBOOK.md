# GyanArt SEO & Organic Lead Generation Playbook

**Version:** 1.0  
**Last reviewed:** 16 November 2026  
**Repository:** gyanart/gyanart  
**Purpose:** A repeatable operating guide for keyword research, page planning, publishing, measurement and organic lead generation.

> **Strategy:** Help small and local businesses build a stronger online presence and generate customer enquiries through website design, SEO, Google Business Profile, digital marketing and creative services.

## 1. Confirmed strategy

| Decision | Direction |
|---|---|
| Audience | Small and local businesses |
| Geography | Bengaluru first, then pan-India |
| Services | Website design/development, SEO, Google Business Profile, digital/performance marketing, graphic design/branding |
| Primary objective | Qualified enquiries and paying clients |
| Content sequence | Commercial service pages → industry pages → informational content → selective locality expansion |

Traffic and page count are not the goal by themselves. Prioritise qualified leads, relevance and useful content.

## 2. Operating principles

1. One dominant search intent should normally map to one preferred URL.
2. Supporting keyword variations belong on the same page when the intent is the same.
3. Do not create pages for synonyms or minor wording changes.
4. Protect existing URLs that have impressions, clicks, backlinks or established indexation. Audit the sitemap and Search Console before creating, renaming or redirecting URLs.
5. Do not mass-produce near-identical city, locality or industry pages. Every page must add unique value.
6. Publish only services GyanArt can actually deliver. Do not invent client logos, reviews, testimonials, case studies, performance claims or guarantees.
7. AI may assist research and drafting, but every page requires human review, evidence, original insight and editorial quality.
8. Every commercial page needs a clear enquiry path and working conversion tracking.
9. Search volume, paid competition and organic ranking difficulty are different metrics. Never label paid competition as organic keyword difficulty.
10. Proposed keywords are hypotheses until validated.

## 3. Website architecture

- **Home:** broad GyanArt proposition and links into service areas.
- **Services:** website design/development, SEO, local SEO, Google Business Profile, digital marketing, paid ads, graphic design and branding.
- **Industries:** dental, clinic/healthcare, restaurant/café, real estate and consultant websites.
- **Locations:** Bengaluru core page first; locality pages only where there is genuine local relevance.
- **Resources / Blog:** website costs, planning, SEO, Google visibility, lead generation and industry guides.
- **Proof:** portfolio, case studies, testimonials and measurable outcomes where genuine and authorised.

Homepage should not target every service keyword. Each page should have a distinct audience, intent, purpose and next step.

## 4. Keyword research and validation workflow

### Step 1 — Search Console baseline
Export 16 months of Performance → Search results → Queries and Pages. Include clicks, impressions, CTR, average position, country and device. Find:
- Queries already producing impressions.
- Pages ranking around positions 4–20.
- High-impression / low-CTR opportunities.
- Search queries that do not have a suitable landing page.
- Bengaluru versus rest-of-India performance.

### Step 2 — Google Keyword Planner
Research each cluster separately. Run India and Bengaluru targeting independently. Use seed keywords and GyanArt's website as inputs. Record average monthly searches, trend, advertiser competition and top-of-page bid ranges. Bid/competition metrics describe advertising markets, not organic SEO difficulty.

### Step 3 — Live SERP review
Search the exact phrase with location context. Review the first page of organic results and local pack where relevant. Record page type, intent, competitors, directory/aggregator presence, quality gaps, freshness and whether a small agency can realistically compete.

### Step 4 — Existing URL and cannibalisation audit
Compare proposed URLs with the live sitemap, indexed URLs and Search Console page-query pairs. Keep established URLs with value. Consolidate pages that target the same intent. Do not create a second page for an existing topic without a clear reason.

### Step 5 — Map and prioritise
Assign one primary keyword and one dominant intent to one preferred URL. Map close variants as supporting terms. Score:
- Commercial intent: 30%
- Relevance to GyanArt: 25%
- Search demand: 20%
- Ranking opportunity: 15%
- Ability to create original content: 10%

These weights are a planning framework, not a measured ranking formula.

### Step 6 — Publish and measure
Track indexation, impressions, clicks, CTR, position, enquiries, qualified leads and lead-to-client outcomes. Review at 28, 56 and 90 days. Prioritise qualified enquiries over raw traffic.

### Keyword validation status
Use: **Proposed → Validating → Validated → Briefed → Drafting → Reviewed → Published → Indexed → Optimising**.

A keyword is not “Validated” until search demand, SERP intent and overlap with existing GyanArt pages have been checked.

### Internal linking standards
- Blog posts link to the most relevant service or industry page where useful.
- Service pages link to relevant industry pages, case studies and supporting guides.
- Industry pages link to matching services and useful industry articles.
- Use natural descriptive anchor text; do not repeat exact-match anchors mechanically.
- Avoid orphan pages. Every indexable page should have at least one relevant internal link.
- Do not link every article to every service. Keep links contextual.

## 7. Page production standards

### Commercial service page
1. Clear H1 describing service and intended customer.
2. Customer problem, desired outcome and value proposition.
3. Who the service is for and when it is appropriate.
4. Deliverables, scope, inclusions and exclusions.
5. Process from discovery through delivery and support.
6. Genuine portfolio, case study or testimonial evidence where approved.
7. Pricing guidance or cost factors where appropriate.
8. FAQs based on real customer questions.
9. Clear enquiry CTA and contact options.
10. Relevant internal links and descriptive image alt text.

### Industry page
- Explain the industry's specific website and customer-acquisition needs.
- Describe relevant features and workflows (appointments, menus, listings, enquiries).
- Use genuine examples or clearly label illustrative examples.
- Include sector-specific FAQs and link to the matching service.
- Do not promise guaranteed patient, customer or revenue outcomes.

### Informational article
- Answer the main question directly near the beginning.
- Use a logical H2/H3 structure and practical examples.
- Include India/Bengaluru context only when it materially changes the answer.
- Explain trade-offs and costs where relevant.
- Add original checklists, tables, examples or visuals.
- Link naturally to one or two relevant commercial pages.
- End with a useful next step, not an aggressive sales pitch.

## 8. Geographic expansion rules

1. **Bengaluru core:** Build substantive website design, SEO and local SEO pages with clear coverage and genuine proof.
2. **Industry pages:** Dental, clinic and restaurant first.
3. **Pan-India:** Build national service/education pages only where remote delivery and national intent are clear.
4. **Locality expansion:** Whitefield, HSR Layout, Electronic City and Koramangala only when unique local value and demand are demonstrated.
5. **Other cities:** Mysuru and others only if GyanArt genuinely serves those markets.

Every locality page must have distinct useful information, real service coverage and local relevance. If it is merely a city-name swap, do not publish it.

## 11. Quality and risk controls

- No keyword stuffing or repetitive city/service combinations.
- No mass-generated near-duplicate locality or industry pages.
- No fabricated testimonials, reviews, client logos, case studies or performance claims.
- No fake Google reviews or review incentives that violate platform policy.
- No guaranteed ranking, traffic, patient, sales or lead promises.
- No separate page for every keyword variation.
- No publishing without checking existing URLs, canonicals, redirects and internal links.
- No deleting or changing established URLs without a migration/redirect plan.
- No thin AI-generated articles; human review and original value are required.
- No untracked forms, WhatsApp links or calls on commercial pages.

## 12. Repeatable page journey checklist

### Discover
- [ ] Keyword sourced from customer question, GSC, Keyword Planner or SERP research.
- [ ] Search intent and audience understood.
- [ ] Service relevance and delivery capability confirmed.

### Validate
- [ ] Search-demand evidence recorded.
- [ ] SERP competitors and page types reviewed.
- [ ] Existing URL/cannibalisation check completed.
- [ ] P1/P2/P3 priority assigned.

### Plan
- [ ] Primary keyword and supporting variants assigned.
- [ ] One preferred URL selected.
- [ ] Page brief, CTA and internal links defined.
- [ ] Evidence, examples and assets identified.

### Create
- [ ] Content answers target intent.
- [ ] Claims and facts checked.
- [ ] Title, meta description, H1 and headings reviewed.
- [ ] Mobile layout, forms and links tested.

### Publish
- [ ] URL live and returns intended status.
- [ ] Canonical and indexability checked.
- [ ] Sitemap updated if required.
- [ ] Internal links added.
- [ ] Conversion events tested.

### Measure and improve
- [ ] Indexing checked in Search Console.
- [ ] Query and page performance reviewed.
- [ ] Qualified enquiries and lead quality recorded.
- [ ] Refresh, consolidate or defer decision documented.

## 13. Inputs required to move from proposed to evidence-based

- GSC Queries and Pages exports for the last 16 months.
- Current XML sitemap and URL inventory.
- Keyword Planner exports for India and Bengaluru.
- Confirmed service catalogue and delivery capacity.
- Genuine portfolio/case studies/testimonials with permission.
- Analytics and enquiry tracking status.
- Canonical host preference (www or non-www) and current redirect rules.

## 14. Monthly review and change log

At each monthly review:
1. Update GSC metrics and lead outcomes.
2. Validate new keyword opportunities and emerging customer questions.
3. Review pages with impressions but weak CTR or ranking.
4. Check cannibalisation and indexation.
5. Decide which pages to create, improve, consolidate or defer.
6. Update this playbook and the companion keyword workbook.
7. Record date, owner, evidence source and reason for every material change.
