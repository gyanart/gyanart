Conversion & UX · Field guide

Why customers find your business but don't contact you

Visibility gets a potential customer to your digital doorstep. It doesn't automatically give them enough confidence to take the next step.

Look for friction across three moments

1. Can people understand the offer? Explain who you help, what you do, where you operate and what makes your service relevant. Avoid vague claims that could describe any competitor.

2. Can people trust what they see? Keep contact details consistent, show real work or genuine examples, answer common questions and make credentials or policies easy to find. Never invent testimonials or results to fill a gap.

3. Is the next step obvious? Make calling, WhatsApp, booking or requesting a quote easy on mobile. Explain what happens after someone contacts you, and avoid asking for unnecessary information.

A quick self-audit

  • Open your site on a phone and try to contact the business with one hand.
  • Ask someone unfamiliar with the business to explain the offer after a five-second glance.
  • Check whether each important page has one clear next action.
  • Track meaningful actions—calls, form submissions and WhatsApp clicks—not just visits.

Takeaway: Before buying more traffic, remove the avoidable friction that prevents existing visitors from becoming enquiries.

Local SEO · Field guide

Your Google Business Profile is part of your website

For a local business, many customers form their first impression on Google Search or Maps before they ever visit the website.

Make the profile useful, not just complete

Use the real-world business name and the most accurate primary category. Keep address or service-area details, opening hours, phone number, website and appointment links current. Describe services in plain language customers recognise.

Use recent, genuine photos that help people understand the location, team, work or experience. Review the questions customers ask over the phone and make the answers easier to find in your profile or website.

Build a responsible review habit

Invite customers to share honest feedback after a real interaction. Respond professionally, protect private customer information and do not offer rewards for positive reviews. A thoughtful response can help future customers understand how the business handles feedback.

Measure actions and quality

Monitor available profile interactions, calls, website visits and direction requests. Where possible, compare these with actual enquiries and completed work. A click is a useful signal, but it is not the same as a qualified lead or sale.

Takeaway: Treat your Business Profile as an active customer touchpoint that needs regular maintenance, not a one-time setup task.

Search & AI · Field guide

Make your business easier to understand in search

Search experiences are changing, but the foundation remains useful information that clearly answers real customer questions.

Start with clarity and consistency

Use the same accurate business name, contact details, service descriptions and locations across your website and legitimate business profiles. Give each important service its own useful explanation rather than relying on a long generic list.

Write headings around the questions customers actually ask: what a service includes, who it is for, how the process works, what affects cost and how to get started. Answer directly, then provide the detail needed to make a good decision.

Make important facts visible

Keep essential information in accessible page text, use descriptive page titles and internal links, and add appropriate structured data only when it accurately reflects visible content. Structured data can help machines interpret a page; it does not guarantee rankings or AI citations.

Prioritise usefulness over tricks

Avoid publishing large volumes of repetitive pages just to target keywords. Create content from genuine expertise, update outdated details and make it easy for a reader to verify important claims.

Takeaway: Optimise first for a customer who needs a reliable answer. Clear structure and accurate information also make your content easier for search systems to interpret.

Website strategy · Field guide

Don't just build a website. Design the customer journey.

A polished interface matters, but a business website should help the right person move from a question to a confident next step.

Design around customer decisions

Begin by identifying the audience, the problem they need solved, the questions that block a decision and the action the business wants to make easy. Then organise pages around those needs.

A service page should explain the problem addressed, what is included, how the process works, relevant proof and how to enquire. A contact page should provide working contact options and set expectations. Navigation should help people orient themselves, especially on deeper pages.

Reduce unnecessary effort

Prioritise mobile readability, fast loading, accessible contrast, descriptive links, simple forms and clear error messages. Remove decorative elements that compete with the main action, and do not force visitors to hunt for phone numbers, service areas or opening hours.

Learn after launch

Use analytics and direct customer feedback to find friction. Track actions that matter to the business, test one meaningful improvement at a time and compare results over a suitable period. Do not assume a redesign has worked simply because it looks newer.

Takeaway: The strongest website is not necessarily the one with the most features. It is the one that helps the intended customer make progress.
