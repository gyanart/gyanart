01 / Context and challenge

Give customers a reason to visit—and an easy way to get there.

Mayura Cafe and Food Court is a neighbourhood restaurant near the BDA Complex, Nagarbhavi. Its menu spans South Indian tiffin, dosa, quick bites and drinks. The restaurant owner was actively involved in the website project and approved showcasing the project with GyanArt.

The agreed primary objective: help people find the restaurant and visit in person. We prioritised clear location information and directions without hiding the food experience.

Design hypothesis, not a research finding: Visitors may find it easier to plan a visit when they can immediately recognise Mayura, check its hours and open the correct navigation destination. We have not conducted independent customer interviews or measured this effect.
02 / Stakeholder discovery

Documented decisions, not imagined personas.

03 / Proposed customer journey

From online discovery to a possible in-person visit.

The journey below visualises the experience we designed for: Discover → Recognise → Plan → Navigate → Arrive. The website supports the journey through the directions action; actual arrival happens outside the site and has not been measured.

04 / User flow

Food browsing should never become a dead end.

Visitors may want directions immediately or explore the menu first. Both paths lead back to the same clear Get Directions action and confirmed Maps location.

05 / Information architecture

A simple structure around the next customer action.

Mayura's homepage is organised around identity, verified visit information, browsable menu categories and directions. We chose editable food names and provisional prices instead of asking visitors to zoom into photographed menu boards.

The menu structure drew on available evidence: South Indian breakfast, dosa and rotti, quick bites, pizzas and sandwiches, juices and shakes, and vegetarian starters. Prices remained provisional, with no unverified GST statement.

06 / Wireframes

Put the important information first—on mobile and desktop.

The early direction prioritised Mayura's name, local landmark, opening hours and a prominent Get Directions action, with food browsing close behind.

Artifact provenance: These are newly drawn reconstructions of the documented V1 planning decisions, not original saved Figma files and not usability-tested wireframes.
07 / Design iterations

Review, refine and make the design work.

The direction changed as feedback arrived. The reason for each iteration matters as much as the visual differences.

V1 · Structure

Clarity first

A quiet green-and-cream homepage established visit-first content and a text-based menu.

V2 · Visual exploration

More food appeal

Added illustrative food cards and category controls while keeping directions prominent.

V2.1 · Functionality

Responsive interactions

Fixed standalone script loading and verified menu switching and mobile navigation.

V2.2 · Visual refinement

Smoother photo blend

After review, a responsive gradient softened the storefront photograph's edge. The updated live appearance was approved.

Images illustrating food are labelled as illustrations where they are not verified photos of dishes served at Mayura.

08 / Build and validation

From interface prototype to publicly testable website.

GyanArt developed the responsive homepage in HTML, CSS and JavaScript, with menu-category interaction, mobile navigation, directions actions, copyable address, keyboard focus treatment and consideration for reduced motion.

The website was committed through a dedicated GitHub repository and transferred to the hosting server using GitHub Actions over FTP/FTPS. Browser tests checked desktop, tablet and mobile widths, image loading, JavaScript errors and horizontal overflow.

Verified technical result: An independent GitHub-hosted HTTPS check returned HTTP 200 and found the confirmed location, opening hours and deployed photo-blending CSS in the public HTML. This does not establish successful user journeys or additional restaurant visits.
09 / Current outcome and limits

A public design preview, with honest next steps.

What exists: a publicly accessible responsive design preview, working directions-first homepage, menu draft, documented stakeholder decisions, accepted design refinements and verifiable technical deployment.

Still pending: final owner approval of image rights and menu/GST information, alignment of older Menu/About/Visit pages, real-participant usability sessions, tracking of direction clicks and a decision to make this the restaurant's official live website.

What we are not claiming: improved rankings, more customers, more physical visits, revenue gains or quantified usability improvement. No such outcomes have been measured.

Until the restaurant officially signs off, its public website remains labelled a design preview and uses a noindex directive. The GyanArt case study itself documents the in-progress work.
