What changes the cost?

Scope matters more than the page count alone.

Number and type of pages

A simple company profile differs from a site with many service pages, product catalogues or location-specific content.

Content and assets

Existing copy, photography and brand assets can reduce preparation work. Research, copywriting and original photography may require separate scope.

Features and integrations

Booking, payments, CRM connections, custom forms, multilingual content and third-party tools add planning, implementation and testing.

Hosting and ongoing support

Domain renewal, hosting, maintenance, updates and paid tools may be separate recurring costs. Ask who owns each account and what support includes.

Before you accept a quote

Ask these questions before comparing prices.

  1. How many pages and what page types are included?
  2. Who writes and approves the website content?
  3. Are domain, hosting, email and paid plugins included or separate?
  4. Are mobile layouts, accessibility and performance checks part of delivery?
  5. Who owns the domain, hosting account, source files and analytics?
  6. Are SEO basics, redirects, Search Console and analytics setup included?
  7. What happens when you need updates after launch?

Compare proposals by deliverables, ownership, exclusions and support—not just the headline price.
